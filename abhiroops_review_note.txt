Based on the discussion, these are the key pointers/advice given for the project:

1. Rework the overall solution flow first

* Do not continue building the UI immediately.
* Create a strawman/conceptual flow first showing:
    * Entry point
    * Customer segmentation
    * Risk identification
    * Customer 360
    * Recommended actions
    * Outreach/recovery
    * Outcome tracking
* This can be a PPT, Excel, flowchart, or even a hand-drawn diagram/photo.
* The objective is to ensure the complete journey is logically connected before more UI development.

2. Redesign the first page / landing page

* The current first page is not making good use of the available real estate.
* It looks more like a basic report than a solution/application.
* Make it more dynamic and action-oriented with:
    * Customer risk segments
    * High/medium/low-risk distribution
    * Key business KPIs
    * Dynamic charts
    * Important insights/actions
* The first screen should immediately answer: “What should I focus on?”

3. Replace the current customer tab with segmentation

Instead of immediately showing individual customers:

* Start with customer segments/portfolios.
* Examples:
    * High Risk
    * Medium Risk
    * Low Risk
    * Other relevant business segments
* Show metrics at the segment level, such as:
    * Number of customers
    * Risk %
    * Recovery opportunity
    * Conversion/recovery rate
    * Relevant business KPIs

4. Use risk as the first filter

The proposed customer navigation should be:

Risk Segment → Customer Name → Customer 360

For example:

* Select High Risk
* System shows the relevant customer population
* Select a customer
* Then open that customer’s detailed Customer 360

This is similar to filtering in Excel and should be intuitive.

5. Customer 360 should be used for individual-level understanding

Once a customer is selected, show:

* Customer attributes
* Risk score
* Relevant history
* Products/services
* Previous interactions
* Recovery/outreach history
* Other information needed to understand the customer

The purpose is to answer:

“What should I do for this customer?”

6. Create a separate “Suggested Actions / Next Best Action” layer

This was one of the biggest recommendations.

The solution should identify:

* What action to take
* Which customer segment
* Which channel
* Which product/service
* When to contact
* What message/offer to use
* Potentially why the action is recommended

This should not be buried inside Customer 360.

7. Do not define actions at individual-customer level

A major point raised was that individual-level action management will not be scalable.

Instead:

* Define rules/actions at segment level
* Example:
    * High-risk customers → SMS + email campaign
    * Specific segment → particular offer
    * Another segment → dealer follow-up
* Once the rule is defined, it should automatically apply to all customers belonging to that segment.

So the model should be:

Segment → Rule → Action/Campaign → Customers

rather than:

Customer → Manually decide action

8. Recovery should be treated as a separate solution area

The discussion suggested that Recovery itself is large enough to become a separate solution/module.

If included in the current story, keep it clearly structured:

* Recovery opportunities
* Actions taken
* Outreach
* Recovery outcomes
* Business impact

Eventually, this could become a dedicated Recovery Solution.

9. Track recovery/outreach outcomes

The recovery section should track execution and results, including:

* Number of SMS sent
* Number of emails sent
* Outreach volume
* Tickets/actions executed
* Execution time
* Follow-ups in the last 3 days
* Customer response
* Recovery outcome
* Business impact

The goal is to answer:

“What actions did we take, and what did we achieve?”

10. Keep business logic and intelligence as the core

The UI is only the facade/interface.

The important part is:

* Why the customer is classified as high risk
* Why a particular action is recommended
* What intelligence/model supports the recommendation
* Why this matters to dealers/business users
* How the solution converts data into a decision

The project should therefore be business-intelligence-led, not UI-led.

11. Make the solution scalable and automation-ready

The design should not look like a one-time report.

It should be built as a solution layer that can later be automated and expanded:

* Model/risk layer
* Segmentation layer
* Decision/action layer
* Execution layer
* Outcome layer

The architecture should allow additional campaigns, segments, products, and actions to be added later.

12. Focus on portfolio/segment-level decision making

For management and business users:

* Avoid showing too much individual-level detail initially.
* Focus on portfolio/segment-level insights and actions.
* Individual customer details should be available through filtering/drill-down.

This makes the solution more manageable and usable.

13. Validate the logic with business/domain experts

The team emphasized that the knowledge and intelligence must come from the team/business experts, not just the UI.

You need to validate:

* Risk logic
* Segmentation
* Dealer perspective
* Recommended actions
* Recovery logic
* Business relevance

The solution should be “airtight” enough that the team can confidently explain why every major recommendation exists.

14. Work iteratively — don’t rush the UI

The feedback was essentially:

* Don’t worry that the first version needs major changes.
* This is an iterative process.
* Better to spend more time getting the concept right than quickly polishing an incorrect UI.
* The target should be a holistic, robust solution, not just something visually impressive.

Immediate next step

The most important action before the next UI build is:

Create a one-page strawman flow showing the complete solution journey:

Risk Model → Customer Segments → Segment Selection → Customer 360 → Next Best Action → Campaign/Outreach → Recovery → Outcome/Impact

Then use that flow to restructure the existing tabs and UI.
